Two different job markets

Experienced engineers

Software postings are up ~15% since early 2025, and 71% of that increase is senior roles. Engineering hiring at big tech is down only 11% vs 2019, and up 7% at startups.

New grads & juniors

Hit hardest. New-grad hiring at big tech is down ~65% vs 2019, and only 4.5% of software postings are entry level. Employment of 22–25 year olds in AI-exposed jobs is 19% below where it would otherwise be.

This is why we hear "nobody's hiring" and "we can't find anyone" in the same week.

SignalFire 2026 · Stanford DEL, Aug 2026 · Indeed Hiring Lab

Sources: SignalFire State of Talent 2026 (Jun 22 2026): new-grad/entry-level hiring down roughly 65% at the Tech Majors and ~76% at early-stage startups vs 2019; overall big-tech hiring 25% below 2019 but engineering hiring down only 11% (and up 7% at startups); engineering is now 55% of Tech Majors hiring, up from 46%. Indeed Hiring Lab (Jul 23 2026): in Q1 2026, 69.3% of software postings were senior and 4.5% entry level. Stanford Digital Economy Lab, Brynjolfsson, Chandar & Chen, "Canaries in the Coal Mine?" (revised Aug 2026): employment of 22–25 year olds in AI-exposed occupations is 19% below where it would be had it kept pace with less-exposed peers, and experienced workers show no comparable gap. Indeed Hiring Lab (Jul 2026): 71% of the increase in software postings from May 2025 to May 2026 came from senior roles.

Also from Indeed: 37% of the posting increase is roles with AI in the title, and AI-related postings are 7.3% of all postings (Oct 2026), more than double the previous peak. Those are new roles where nobody has ten years of experience, which is an opening for people early in their careers.

The pictures: the joke on the juniors side is on job ads, not on juniors. Everyone has seen a "junior" role asking for five years of experience.
