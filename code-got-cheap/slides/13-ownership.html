Anthropic, Jan 2026

Four clicks: the git blame half, the 2am half, why it feels like nobody, then the rule of thumb.

The study: Anthropic, "How AI assistance impacts the formation of coding skills" (Jan 29 2026). 52 mostly junior engineers learned Trio, a Python library new to all of them. The AI group averaged 50% on the comprehension quiz afterwards, the hand-coding group 67% (p = 0.01). The AI group was only about two minutes faster, and that wasn't significant. The biggest gap was on debugging questions, which is exactly what you need at 2am.

The nuance worth saying out loud: how people used AI mattered more than whether they did. People who used it to ask questions and get explanations scored 65% or more. People who handed it the writing and debugging scored under 40%.

On the left, give them a second to read the "TODO: why 0.37?" line. Everyone has merged a magic number they didn't choose.

The ownership question. When nobody really wrote it, does anybody really understand it? This shows up at 2am in an incident, when the code that broke is code no human on the team has a mental model of.

What good teams do: the person who merges owns it, full stop. "The AI wrote it" is never an answer in a postmortem, any more than "Stack Overflow wrote it" was.
