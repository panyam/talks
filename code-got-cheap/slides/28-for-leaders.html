AI rollout checklist
  • Know what you want built. More capacity doesn't help if the goal isn't clear. DHH
  • Fix the system first. AI speeds up the process you already have. DORA
  • Budget for review. Plan reviewer time like any other capacity. Orosz
  • Keep hiring juniors. They're your seniors in five years.
  • Hire for judgment. Can they tell when the AI is wrong?
  • Pair mandates with enablement. Only 17% of developers say their org has a clear AI strategy. Atlassian
☕ owner: you

The counterpart to slide 32, for the managers and hiring managers in the room. Six clicks, each ticks a box. About 90 seconds, so say one line per item and move on.

Know what you want built: DHH (Lex Fridman #501) says most organisations are "bottlenecked on ideas… on vision… on taste", so adding implementation capacity doesn't help on its own. This is about organisations, not career advice for individuals.

Fix the system first: DORA's amplifier finding (slide 10). Fast CI, small PRs and clear ownership are where the gains come from.

Budget for review: Orosz, "The state of the tech industry in 2026" (Oct 6), "no engineer can keep up with 5-10 times more code to review", his own narration.

Keep hiring juniors: slide 16. Pair them with people as well as with AI.

Hire for judgment: Orosz quotes an anonymous Director of Engineering at a Series D startup, "AI positivity was something we started to screen for in our hiring process". Alongside AI fluency, check that people can tell when the AI is wrong.

Mandates: Charity Majors, "In defense of AI mandates" (Jul 2026), "A mandate is a forcing function for identifying a timeline and figuring out what kind of enablement is needed." Atlassian DevEx 2026: only 17% of developers and 23% of leaders say their org has a clear AI strategy.

If asked about measurement: Laura Tacho (DX) reportedly shared data in Feb 2026 showing ~93% of developers using AI with productivity gains around 10%, and called it "really a management problem" (as reported by ShiftMag, I haven't seen the primary).
